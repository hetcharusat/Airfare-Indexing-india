Chapter 1
The Goal
1.1 What We Are Actually Building
We are building APIx (Airfare Price Index) : a system that scrapes Indian airline and OTA
websites daily, and turns those noisy, dynamic-priced, partially-missing observations into a statisti-
cally defensible index of how much flying actually costs in India — separated cleanly from changes
in what happened to be observed .
The one-sentence goal:
Build the first Indian airfare index that can tell the difference between “fares actually went
up” and “we just happened to see more expensive bookings this month.”
1.2 Why This Problem Statement Exists
The Consumer Price Index (CPI), published monthly by MoSPI/NSO, feeds directly into RBI’s
inflation-targeting monetary policy. The ‘Transport and Communication’ sub-group still collects
air-fare prices largely through manual, limited-outlet collection – even though over 90% of domestic
tickets are now sold online through airlines and OTAs (MakeMyTrip, Yatra, EaseMyTrip, Cleartrip,
Ixigo, Goibibo). Manual collection cannot capture fares that swing 200–400% in a single day
depending on advance-booking window, day-of-week, and demand surges.
The PS explicitly asks for an automated, high-frequency, scalable system that mirrors what a real
Indian traveller actually pays – and asks teams to backtest at least 30 days of results against publicly
available DGCA monthly average-fare data.
1.3 Why Most Teams Will Fail This Problem
Out of an estimated 40+ competing teams on this exact PS, the overwhelming majority will submit
some variation of:
scrape prices→ average them→ put on a dashboard → compare loosely to DGCA
This is not wrong, but it is not an index in any rigorous economic sense. A simple average of
whatever fares you happened to observe conflates two completely different phenomena:
1. Real price movement – fares for the same product (same route, same carrier mix, same
booking window) genuinely got more or less expensive.
2. Composition / mix effects – your average moved because you happened to scrape more
long-haul routes this week, or more people in your sample booked last-minute, or a low-cost

Chapter 2
Our Philosophy
2.1 The Core Insight (Read This Twice)
An index is not an average. An index is a controlled comparison.
The entire discipline of index-number theory (Laspeyres, Paasche, Fisher – all over a century
old) exists to solve exactly one problem: how do you compare a “basket” of prices across time
when the basket itself keeps changing? CPI, WPI, and every serious price index in the world
is built this way. Nobody scrapes prices and simply averages them and calls it inflation. We
refuse to do that either.
This is our entire intellectual position, and it is the single thread that must run through every
slide, every line of code, and every answer we give a judge. We are not a scraping project with a
dashboard bolted on. We are an index-construction project that happens to need a scraper to get
raw material.
2.2 Why This Is Defensible, Not Just “Sounds Smart”
We are explicitly not claiming to have invented a new algorithm. Laspeyres indexing is 100+ year
old economics. Our defensibility does not come from novelty of method – it comes from being the
only team that correctly applied a known, rigorous method to a domain that currently has none.
What we are NOT claiming:
 We are NOT claiming our index is more “accurate” than DGCA’s in some absolute sense.
 We are NOT claiming AI/ML makes this novel – we use standard statistics correctly, not
buzzwords.
 We are NOT claiming our scraper is technologically special.
What we ARE claiming:
 We correct a real, quantifiable, explainable methodological error that a naive average
makes.
 We can show, with our own data, exactly how large that error is.
 We can explain, in one sentence, why a fixed basket + lead-time stratification produces
a more economically meaningful number.
2.3 The Three Pillars
Pillar 1: Fixed Basket, Not Free Sampling. We decide, in advance, which routes and
which booking-window buckets constitute “the basket.” We never let the basket

Chapter 3
Key Terms (The Words You Need to
Actually Know)
This is the glossary. If you cannot explain every term in this chapter, in your own words, without
looking at this document, you are not ready to present. A judge will use exactly this vocabulary to
attack you.
Airfare Price Index (APIx)
Our project’s name for the number we produce daily: a single figure (or set of figures per
route/segment) tracking how the cost of flying in India is changing over time, expressed rela-
tive to a base value of 100 on day one.
Basket
The fixed set of routes (e.g., DEL-BOM, DEL-BLR, BOM-BLR, DEL-CCU, BLR-HYD, MAA-
DEL) and booking-window buckets we track every single day, chosen once at the start using
DGCA passenger-traffic data, and never silently changed.
Booking Window / Lead-Time Bucket
How many days before departure the fare is observed: T+1 (booking tomorrow), T+7, T+15,
T+30, T+45. A T+1 fare and a T+45 fare for the identical flight are economically different
products – one is distress/last-minute pricing, the other is advance-planning pricing. Pooling
them together is a category error.
Composition Effect / Mix Effect
The part of an observed price change that comes purely from which flights you happened to
sample – not from any actual price movement. Example: if this week you accidentally scraped
more premium long-haul fares than last week, your raw average goes up even if every individual
fare stayed exactly the same.
Laspeyres Index
An index formula that computes the cost of a fixed basket of goods (here: routes × booking-
windows) using current prices, always weighted by base-period quantities/weights. This is pre-
cisely the tool that prevents composition effects from contaminating the number. It is the same
family of method used to construct CPI itself.
Normalization
The process of stripping out taxes, User Development Fee (UDF), and convenience charges so
that what remains – the base fare – is genuinely comparable across different OTAs and airlines
that display these components differently.
Outlier / Missing-Data Handling
Rules for excluding clearly erroneous observations (e.g. business-class fares mixed into an economy

Bootstrap Confidence Interval
A resampling technique that lets us report our index not as a single bare number, but as a number

Decomposition

inflation, how much from route-mix shift, how much from booking-window mix shift. This is the
single chart that makes our submission different from every naive scrape-and-average team.
DGCA Reference Comparison
Comparing our daily/weekly index against DGCA’s publicly released monthly average domestic
fares – explicitly framed as contextual validation, never as proof of correctness, since DGCA’s
own methodology (unstratified, monthly, broad averaging) is structurally different from ours.
Ethical / Rate-Limited Scraping
Collecting data while respecting robots.txt, using reasonable request rates, and avoiding ag-
gressive anti-bot circumvention – because the PS explicitly requires this, and because a judge can
and will ask about it.

4.2 Stage-by-Stage Breakdown
4.2.1 Stage 1 — Multi-Source Scraping
Collect fares daily from major airlines (IndiGo, Air India, Air India Express, Akasa Air, SpiceJet)
and leading OTAs, across the fixed basket of routes and lead-time buckets. Technology choice
(Scrapy, Selenium, Playwright) is secondary to consistency: the scraper must run every single day
without gaps, because gaps are the single biggest threat to backtesting credibility.
4.2.2 Stage 2 — Cleaning & Normalization
Split base fare from taxes/UDF/convenience fees. Drop cancelled/sold-out listings. Flag and handle
missing route×window combinations rather than silently ignoring them.

Bootstrap a confidence band around the corrected index. Decompose any observed change into:
real price movement vs. route-mix effect vs. booking-window-mix effect.

Overlay DGCA’s monthly average fares against our daily/weekly index, and explicitly explain any
divergence via known methodological differences (stratification vs. no stratification) – rather than
claiming alignment proves correctness.

Chapter 6
Judge Defense Script
These are rehearsed, verbatim-ready answers to the most likely hostile questions. Every team
member should be able to deliver these without hesitation.
Q: “Isn’t this just a scraper with a dashboard?”
A: “A scraper reports what prices were observed. We report what actually changed – holding
the observation mix constant. Here’s the gap between the naive number and our corrected
number, and here’s exactly why that gap exists.”
Q: “How do you know your composition correction is even right?”
A: “We fix the basket and its weights before collection starts – exactly the same Laspeyres-
family methodology MoSPI already uses for other CPI sub-groups. We’re extending the same
rigor to a sub-group that currently has none.”
Q: “Your number doesn’t match DGCA’s – so you’re wrong?”
A: “DGCA publishes monthly, unstratified averages. We stratify by booking window because
a T+1 fare and a T+45 fare for the same route are not the same economic product. The
divergence is the finding, not the error.”
Q: “With 40+ teams on this exact problem, why should we pick you?”
A: “Every other team will show you an average. We are the only team that can show you the
difference between an average and an index – and quantify, in points, how much that difference
is worth.”
